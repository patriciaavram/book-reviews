Good Girl Bad Blood

by Holly Jackson

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

Pip is not a detective anymore. With the help of Ravi Singh, she released a true-crime podcast about the murder case they solved together last year. The podcast has gone viral, yet Pip insists her investigating days are behind her. But she will have to break that promise when someone she knows goes missing. Jamie Reynolds has disappeared, on the very same night the town hosted a memorial for the sixth-year anniversary of the deaths of Andie Bell and Sal Singh. The police won't do anything about it. And if they won't look for Jamie then Pip will, uncovering more of her town's dark secrets along the way... and this time everyone is listening. But will she find him before it's too late?

My Thoughts

My god, WHAT a sequel to Pip's story. Just when I thought this poor girl couldn't go through more, life keeps throwing more at her. She said it would be her last case, never again taking another. But she did what any good friend with experience in this sort of thing would do to help another friend. I respect her a lot for that. Where do I even start discussing this book... Pip, Ravi, Jamie, Connor, Charlie, Stanley were all incredibly well written and all layered characters that brought so much color to this book. Also it's not often that I read a sequel that is just as good and as gripping as the first novel, but in this case it was absolutely true. I could not put this book down and was on the edge of my seat the whole time.

I felt like the pacing was good but sometimes the reveals felt a little rushed, especially the last reveal of Stanley's past. Had to take breaks and reread a couple of times to really connect all the dots in this story. I will say that I was suspicious of the introduction of the Child Brunswick case that was slowly getting introduced throughout the book in sprinkles and mentions. This was especially true in the TV show adaptation, where we see Pip watching a documentary about it before Jamie even goes missing, but as an outsider who hadn't read the book beforehand, I wouldn't have been able to tell that this was foreshadowing. I understand both sides of the Charlie/Stanley conundrum, where Charlie is rightfully upset at Stanley for being the pawn in his father game that led Charlie's sister to be kidnapped and killed. On the other hand, Stanley was a child, and children of that age cannot consent and make their own decisions, as they are completely influenced by outsiders, such as their parents and their actions. In my opinion, both sides are valid but Charlie crossed a line when he killed Stanley. An incredibly well written scene, that got me so emotional that I was fully sobbing. The follow-up of Pip at the police station being in shock was accurately described, and I was actually surprised that Holly Jackson kept Pip's PTSD a present and prominent theme in the third novel. She either really understand or relates, or she has done extensive research on the consequences of being in a traumatic event like this. Either way, props to her, she did an amazing job.

Another thing to mention is that I really liked that Pip kept the same format of investigating as she did in her school project, i.e. showing her interview process, with the transcripts, the pictures of the evidence, the research articles she reads, and that they all help in reaching a conclusion on her side and to support the readers in reaching one on our side. Also, following the whole Max Hastings trial was absolutely heartbreaking and showcases how broken the justice system can be at times. Overall an amazing read, similarly to the first book in the series, which rekindled my love for mysteries.
