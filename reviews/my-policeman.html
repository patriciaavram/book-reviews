My Policeman

by Bethany Roberts

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

From the moment Marion first lays eyes on Tom - her best friend's big brother, broad, blond, blue-eyed - she is smitten. And when he comes home from National Service to be a policeman, Marion, a newly qualified teacher, is determined to win him. Unable to acknowledge the signs that something is amiss, she plunges into marriage, sure that her love is enough for both of them... But Tom has another life, another equally overpowering claim on his affections. Patrick, a curator at the Brighton Museum, is also besotted with his policeman, and opens Tom's eyes to a world previously unknown to him. But in an age when those of 'minority status' were condemned by society and the law, it is safer for this policeman to marry his teacher. The two lovers must share him, until one of them breaks and three lives are destroyed. Unfolding through the dual narratives of Marion and Patrick, both writing about the man at the centre of their lives, this beautifully-told, painful, tragic story is revealed.It is a tale of wasted years, misguided love and thwarted hope, of how at a time when the country was on the verge of change so much was still impossible.

My Thoughts

This story was beautiful and needed to be told from this perspective, as I'm sure that this situation of having an unrequited love, and most importantly how people in the LGBTQ community had to adapt in different times in order to not be arrested, or even killed. I thought this book was beautifully written, and showcased three different sides of the injustice that the characters faced. Patrick obviously felt this injustice the most in his life due to who he was, in his attraction to other men, whereas Marion felt it in her relationship mostly, as well as in societal pressures related to her being a woman and being slightly different from other women (notably taller, with a different hair color, a bit lankier than other women). Also I noticed that the author portrayed Patrick to be a very educated, literate and eloquent man, as opposed to what I imagine were very harsh stereotypes for people like him at the time. I found it overall very easy to connect with both Patrick and Marion's character, but I felt that Tom was a bit detached from the story, almost as if both Patrick and Marion were seeing him as being a god, which is why I couldn't connect with Tom as much as with the two other characters. This also made me feel like Tom wasn't even the main character in this story, as both other characters were more interestingly described and had more depth to them. I found Tom to be quite exploitative of Marion in their relationship, taking advantage of her kindness and lying quite often, as well as abandoning Tom out of fear. I understand his difficulties, but I found him to have treated both Marion and Patrick very poorly. In hindsight, I recognize that he might have been this way because he was panicking about his own situation, not understanding his own feelings and trying to navigate what he was feeling towards both Patrick and Marion. Overall a gorgeous and tragic story, with a unique narrative voice that switches between Marion and Patrick's voices.
