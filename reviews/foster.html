Foster

by Claire Keegan

Rating: ★★★★★

Summary

A small girl is sent to live with foster parents on a farm in rural Ireland, without knowing when she will return home. In the strangers' house, she finds a warmth and affection she has not known before and slowly begins to blossom in their care. And then a secret is revealed and suddenly, she realizes how fragile her idyll is.

Foster - Goodreads

My Thoughts

This novella is a perfect example of how sometimes, simplicity hits the spot better than trying to build a tragic backstory to make people feel empathetic toward the main character. This little girl came from a normal family, had a quiet, albeit busy life helping out her family with household and farming tasks. Yet, something was always missing in her heart, which she found out by the care with which her foster family treated her from the get-go. They did not know her, yet they treated her as their own from the start, and she clearly appreciated the gesture, as she opened up under their care. This is also a story of how parents must shield their children (organic, or fostered) from heartache. For whatever reason beyond the reader's knowledge, the Kinsellas have not revealed the tragic events that happened during their lives that made them lose their son. This story was so rich in its every sentence, that after 84 pages I felt that I had read 300, and it made it hard to let go of this book.

This is the definition of a perfectly written story in my opinion, extremely re-readable since I am sure that I missed some parts of the story and I will learn something new by rereading. Claire Keegan's writing is exquisite, and the way she depicts the irish countryside makes me yearn to live a soft life there alongside the main characters.
