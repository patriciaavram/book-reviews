Small Things Like These

by Claire Keegan

Rating: ★★★★⯪

Summary

It is 1985 in a small Irish town. During the weeks leading up to Christmas, Bill Furlong, a coal merchant and family man faces into his busiest season. Early one morning, while delivering an order to the local convent, Bill makes a discovery which forces him to confront both his past and the complicit silences of a town controlled by the church.

Small Things Like These - Goodreads

My Thoughts

A novel about how small things can alter the course of someone's life. Obviously, the main character, Furlong, saving the young girl from the laundry establishment (where she was forced into free labor) without thinking about how the repercussions will impact his family and reputation was an act of pure selflessness. Even the realiziation that he could have been more selfless for others was touching - he could have given the candies he received from the richer families to the poorer ones for Christmas; his self-reflection on how he could have given the workers of his business more time off for the holidays. He doesn't partake in the town gossip, but nevertheless springs to action whenever people need him. He tries his best to give his family the Christmas gifts that they wish for, even though they're financially tight on money. These small things make a good, decent, modest and compassionate man out of him, and this is a perfect example of a good man in society. On the writing side, Claire Keegan once again proves that a short book doesn't necessarily lack substance. She is able to make every sentence ripe with information, but not so much that the point of the plot is lost on the reader. She is a master at conveying her ideas in sentences that are short & sweet, but that are still beautiful to read.
