Turtles All The Way Down

by John Green

Rating: ★★★★⯪

Summary

Aza Holmes never intended to pursue the disappearance of fugitive billionaire Russell Pickett, but there’s a hundred-thousand-dollar reward at stake and her Best and Most Fearless Friend, Daisy, is eager to investigate. So together, they navigate the short distance and broad divides that separate them from Pickett’s son Davis. Aza is trying. She is trying to be a good daughter, a good friend, a good student, and maybe even a good detective, while also living within the ever-tightening spiral of her own thoughts.

Turtles All The Way Down - Goodreads

My Thoughts

What a novel!! Although it is described as being a young adult novel, the topics were quite comprehensive and did not reduce OCD to simply "overthinking". This makes a lot of sense given John Green's history with OCD and how he is likewise afflicted by it, but it's different reading the words of someone who suffers from it than from someone who has simply done research on it before writing the book. The thoughts feel more deep and it's easier to understand it as it's more concrete. In any case, I thought the characters were elaborate, given that they are teenagers, and all suffer from one thing or another, which makes them relatable.The only thing keeping me from giving this novel a 5 stars is that I felt that some subplots were a bit unnecessary, and some of the teenager lingo was a bit overdone. For instance, I felt that the investigation into Davis' dad became a backstory after he gave them the 10K$ and simply wasn't mentioned until suddenly Daisy and Aza went on a walk after the art show and found a very bad smell that they simply assumed was Davis' father. It just felt a bit clunky to me, but otherwise, the depictions of OCD, and how a chronic illness can complicate friendships and relationships that much was really well described, and a pleasure to read.
