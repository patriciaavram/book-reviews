Yellowface

by R.F. Kuang

Rating: ★★★⯪☆

Summary

Authors Juniper Hayward and Athena Liu were supposed to be twin rising stars. But Athena is a literary darling while June is a nobody. Who wants stories about basic white girls?, June thinks. So when June witnesses Athena’s death in a freak accident, she acts on impulse, stealing Athena’s just-finished masterpiece, an experimental novel about the unsung contributions of Chinese laborers during World War I. So what if June edits Athena’s novel and sends it to her agent as her own work? So what if she lets her new publisher rebrand her as Juniper Song—complete with an ambiguously ethnic author photo? This piece of history deserve to be told, whoever the teller. That is what June believes, and The New York Times bestseller list agrees. But June cannot escape Athena’s shadow, and emerging evidence threatens her stolen success. As she races to protect her secret she discovers exactly how far she will go to keep what she thinks she deserves.

My Thoughts

Quite frankly, this novel felt like more of a commentary on the toxicity of social media within the publishing industry leading to "cancel culture", rather than on the subject of plagiarism and cultural appropriation within the writing industry. I felt that we were being spoon-fed information that didn't really give us a choice in forming an opinion about the characters and about the negative impact of social media on authors. Would've liked to see more of a discussion on those subjects rather than being simply shown the Twittosphere's reactions to June's actions, causing the spiral that led to her downfall. Had a hard time focusing in the middle part of the book when the entire plot was about June's reactions to what she was reading online, all the Twitter comments and the Goodreads reviews could've been shortened. The readers don't need a play by play of what people are saying and who they're quote tweeting or who they write a blog about and what June's reactions are to all those things- at some point it became very repetitive. Anywho, the subject of plagiarizm in the publishing industry is very relevant and in demand currently, especially with all this talk surrounding the use of AI in writing, and the potential impacts of using stolen work to write your own "original" work. So in theory, the premise of this book was really interesting, but in execution there was a lot of social media involved to pass a message that I felt the readers were supposed to figure out ourselves.

I also felt that the supporting characters felt mostly flat, in the way they were acting throughout this story. The most remarkable and layered character in this novel was ironically Athena herself, and as the story unfolded and we learned more about her and her past, I felt annoyed that she was killed off so early in the book, although I will admit that learning all these new things about her through June's perspective made the story more interesting. I will say that R.F. Kuang's writing is very elegant and easy to get through, and the pacing flowed extremely well, and I do like how she developed the internal side of the Athena Liu/June Hayward/Juniper Song interactions, even when Athena began "haunting" after writing The Last Front, it was an unexpected and very welcomed twist to the story.

Although extremely well written, this was a frustrating read at times, bu understandably so given that the author was trying to build an insufferable character in June. June's character was quite annoying and handled everything wrong in this journey of plagiarism, but her mental transformation toward insanity was interesting to see. No matter how everyone in the public sphere reacted to her actions and her books, she always felt like she was in the right to do what she did, which led her to having a internal buildup of anxiety that ultimately leading to her downfall. The scene on the top of the stairs with Candice was especially frustrating to read, and made June seem extremely naïve to me. If you're going to go through all the trouble to plagiarize and appropriate a story that is not yours to tell, then it seems that you should be able to defend/cover yourself and your actions with your whole being, and not just give away your entire story. However, I will say that the plot twist that Candice was the one going after June was very entertaining as well, and I assume this was RFK's attempt at showing how cutthroat the writing sphere is, and it did pass the message pretty well.

Final thoughts: Entertaining book in the sense that it was funny watching June's downfall. Not entertaining in the sense of having half of the book be centered around the Twittosphere and Goodreads reviews shaping the story in an attempt to make readers feel bad for June.
