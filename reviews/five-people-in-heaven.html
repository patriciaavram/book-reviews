The Five People You Meet in Heaven

by Mitch Albom

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

Eddie is a wounded war veteran, an old man who has lived, in his mind, an uninspired life. His job is fixing rides at a seaside amusement park. On his 83rd birthday, a tragic accident kills him as he tries to save a little girl from a falling cart. He awakes in the afterlife, where he learns that heaven is not a destination. It's a place where your life is explained to you by five people, some of whom you knew, others who may have been strangers. One by one, from childhood to soldier to old age, Eddie's five people revisit their connections to him on earth, illuminating the mysteries of his "meaningless" life, and revealing the haunting secret behind the eternal question: "Why was I here?"

My Thoughts

This novel was a short piece of fiction, about a maintenance worker at an amusement park named Eddie, who suddenly dies trying to save a little girl's life due to a malfunctioning ride at the park. It tells a tale of how a single action one takes, like throwing a baseball into the street (as Eddie and his friends did), or one snap decision, like Eddie's commanding officer deciding to shoot him in the leg to aid in their escape from the camp, can have long-lasting effects on either your life or someone else's life. Through this story, we get to know more about the things that have made Eddie's life into what it was at the end, for instance why he chose to stay and work at his father's place of work, at the amusement park. Although some people argue that this book was a failed attempt at conveying deep lessons, I rather think that not every book has to have some earth-shattering lessons to it, and that sometimes just reading about one's life and regrets, and things that they would've done differently, is enough to give us a different perspective on life events. Even though I somewhat anticipated that the last person Eddie met in heaven would not be the little girl he saved, but rather the person he saw in the hut that the soldiers set on fire, I still thoroughly enjoyed the storyline and the writing did make me emotional. Overall, I wouldn't say this is an overly religious book, or that its lessons are corny, but instead this is a short and sentimental story about Eddie's life, his regrets and his moments of happiness.
