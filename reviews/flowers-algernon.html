Flowers for Algernon

by Daniel Keyes

Rating: ★★★★★

Summary

Charlie Gordon is about to embark upon an unprecedented journey. Born with an unusually low IQ, he has been chosen as the perfect subject for an experimental surgery that researchers hope will increase his intelligence - a procedure that has already been highly successful when tested on a lab mouse named Algernon. As the treatment takes effect, Charlie's intelligence expands until it surpasses that of the doctors who engineered his metamorphosis. The experiment appears to be a scientific breakthrough of paramount importance, until Algernon suddenly deteriorates. Will the same happen to Charlie?

My Thoughts

This novel was an incredible story, showcasing the unfortunate testing on disabled humans, in the search for a cure to advance science. This is a story about the circle of life, more than about a simple experiment to defy nature in search of higher intelligence. Charlie starts off as a mentally challenged young man, and in getting the operation he starts to navigate the world as a grown man would, but increasingly learns how to behave as a man and not as a boy. He stops getting favours because people stop pitying him, he learns how to behave in society around people of his age, both men and women. Eventually, his intelligence surpasses him, and he begins learning at a higher rate than his emotions can process, leading to resentment from the people who knew him as a mentally challenged young man at the start. He suddenly has a hard time keeping relationships with these people, and starts mentally regressing as Algernon starts regressing as well. The parallels between Charlie and Algernon were touching, both in their behaviour and in their respective mental conditions, and in how people treated them similarly because of their condition. Overall, this story was a 10/10, and the writing was exquisite; I was entranced the whole time and couldn't put this book down.
