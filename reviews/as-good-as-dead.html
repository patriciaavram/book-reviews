As Good As Dead

by Holly Jackson

Rating: ★★★⯪☆

Summary

By the end of this mystery series, you'll never think of good girls the same way again... Pip is about to head to college, but she is still haunted by the way her last investigation ended. She's used to online death threats in the wake of her viral true-crime podcast, but she can't help noticing an anonymous person who keeps asking her: Who will look for you when you're the one who disappears? Soon the threats escalate and Pip realizes that someone is following her in real life. When she starts to find connections between her stalker and a local serial killer caught six years ago, she wonders if maybe the wrong man is behind bars. Police refuse to act, so Pip has only one choice: find the suspect herself—or be the next victim. As the deadly game plays out, Pip discovers that everything in her small town is coming full circle... and if she doesn't find the answers, this time she will be the one who disappears...

My Thoughts

Unfortunately, the phrase "By the end of this mystery series, you'll never think of good girls the same way again", is correct in the case of this book. I was slightly disappointed by the turn of events in this story. Pip's sweet nature turning out to be hypocritical was not what I expected. But starting from the beginning, the depictions of PTSD were ones of the most realistic I've read in fiction so far. The investigation into the DT killer had me on my seat the entire time, and I was a little suspicious of Jason Bell from the start. I felt that the investigation was rushed, which was definitely the intention so kudos to Holly Jackson for showcasing urgency in the investigation in such a convincing way. The kidnapping and getting away from Jason Bell was an incredible turn of events, and I was on the edge of my seat the entire time as well. She meticulously found a way to protect herself in both cases where she would either have been killed, by planting DNA evidence in the back of Jason's truck, or whether she would've escaped. Unfortunately, I am still torn between understanding her disdain for the police, given all the times they hadn't believed her stories, and thinking she would've been way better off by going to the police instead. I'm leaning toward the latter. Against the numerous evidence she left in the back of Jason's car, she easily would've been able to defend herself. I'm somewhat understandable of her decision to shoot Jason Bell in self-defense, given that it was entirely his fault for putting her in this position. What I don't agree with however, and something that ended up ruining the story for me, was her decision to frame Max Hastings for the murder of Jason Bell. She suddenly became the type of person she would've been investigating, which makes her a hypocrite. I believe that she was going through some sort of a psychotic break when she made this decision, and I believe that Ravi should've been the voice of reason in this case. She needed real support and instead he decided to support her in the wrong way. She might've needed it at the start but they both became just as bad of people as others actually involved in crimes, and arguably put them on the same level as Max Hastings. I have no sympathies for Max, but this took Pip from 'small-town detective' to 'hypocritical criminal'. Overall, slightly disappointing, but the writing and subplots were intricate and intriguing.
