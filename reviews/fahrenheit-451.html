Fahrenheit 451

by Ray Bradbury

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

Nearly seventy years after its original publication, Ray Bradbury’s internationally acclaimed novel Fahrenheit 451 stands as a classic of world literature set in a bleak, dystopian future. Today its message has grown more relevant than ever before. Guy Montag is a fireman. His job is to destroy the most illegal of commodities, the printed book, along with the houses in which they are hidden. Montag never questions the destruction and ruin his actions produce, returning each day to his bland life and wife, Mildred, who spends all day with her television “family.” But when he meets an eccentric young neighbor, Clarisse, who introduces him to a past where people didn’t live in fear and to a present where one sees the world through the ideas in books instead of the mindless chatter of television, Montag begins to question everything he has ever known.

My Thoughts

The premise of this story was extremely interesting and very relevant in this day and age, and the delivery did not disappoint at all. The prose was incredibly well written, the vocabulary being introduced based on this dystopian society is original and yet still very easy to pick up quickly. I loved the chain of events of meeting Clarisse, to seeing a new perspective that she has on the world and on her life, to the murder of the older woman and the burning of her home and her books, to this planting seeds of doubt in his mind about his job and his home life, and finally leading to his newfound rebellion. This was quite eloquantly described by the author, to the point where it felt like it was gradually happening in his brain, as opposed to it happening in clear chunks that we can distinguish.

Additionally, I felt that all the characters were relevant to the story and had their own role to play in Montag's descent into madness. The first seed being planted by Clarisse led him to bringing a book home, the older lady's death and the interrogation of Montag's boss leading him to reconsider his own life path, which led him to notice and question Mildred's behaviour from the moment she overdosed on the sleeping pills. All of these characters, and more, including Prof. Faber, the Mechanical Hound, the kids chasing after him in his car while he was running away, and the vagabonds he meets after fleeing from his crimes, are important to the evolution of the story and furthermore to Montag's evolution as a person. I found the pacing of this novel to be easily followable, and not at all stunted; the story flows well and Montag's descent into rebellion was very smooth until it actually happened. I also did not expect him to actually fall into a murderous rage and hurt Beatty and the other firemen. Overall, an entertaining read and easy to finish in a day, while still keeping its message clear and concise to the readers.
