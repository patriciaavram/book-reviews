This Is How You Lose the Time War

by Amal El-Mohtar

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

Among the ashes of a dying world, an agent of the Commandant finds a letter. It reads: Burn before reading. Thus begins an unlikely correspondence between two rival agents hellbent on securing the best possible future for their warring factions. Now, what began as a taunt, a battlefield boast, grows into something more. Something epic. Something romantic. Something that could change the past and the future. Except the discovery of their bond would mean death for each of them. There's still a war going on, after all. And someone has to win that war.

My Thoughts

I have some conflicting thoughts about this book. First of all, obviously the prose is beautiful and it felt more like reading poetry than reading a novel at times. In my opinion, I felt that the way it was written prevented the story from flowing naturally, just because I was spending more time figuring out where the character actually was, or what the purpose of a chapter was in the overall plot, or what in the world they were doing, or what a specific term from the worldbuilding meant. This was written a bit pretentiously for my taste, and although it was beautiful and it clearly requires immense skill from a writer to make a cohesive text written in this way, and that it could have been a great love story, there was no space left in my brain to actually feel the emotions from this text. I give this book 4 stars just because the writing is excellent, and the plot is unlike anything I've ever read before. The worldbuilding is out of this world (figuratively and literally) and the characters do show character development throughout the book. However, the romance did feel stilted in the sense that the switch from rivalry to love was quite steep, it suddenly just felt as if one of the characters developed feelings and they just rolled with it for the rest of the book. The writing also felt convoluted and often made me so confused that I had to reread segments of a page multiple times before understanding what was happening. Overall, this would have been an amazing poetry collection, but I did not fully get to enjoy this novel because of the reasons listed above.
