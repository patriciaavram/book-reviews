All Boys Aren't Blue

by George M. Johnson

Rating: ★★★★★

Summary

In a series of personal essays, prominent journalist and LGBTQIA+ activist George M. Johnson explores his childhood, adolescence, and college years in New Jersey and Virginia. From the memories of getting his teeth kicked out by bullies at age five, to flea marketing with his loving grandmother, to his first sexual relationships, this young-adult memoir weaves together the trials and triumphs faced by Black queer boys. Both a primer for teens eager to be allies as well as a reassuring testimony for young queer men of color, All Boys Aren't Blue covers topics such as gender identity, toxic masculinity, brotherhood, family, structural marginalization, consent, and Black joy. Johnson's emotionally frank style of writing will appeal directly to young adults.

My Thoughts

I had never read a memoir before and this was a solid start that made me want to read more stories about peoples’ lives written in this fashion. I was moved by George's journey and how he learned to find himself through good and bad experiences in his life, within his community and in school. The support he received from his family further enforces my theory that being raised in a respectful and supportive family or environment can only make you grow deeper as a person. I found George's bond with his grandmother to be very moving, as I myself have a similar bond with my grandmother, even though she lives an ocean away. I’ve never felt like I was too much, or felt like a nuisance, and she’s only showered me with her love and support all throughout her life. George's narration style was also very refreshing and easy to follow, which I was very appreciative of, seeing as certain memoirs have a linear narration, compared to this story being split by specific moments of events in his life. Those linear narrations of more classic memoirs can make it difficult to stay engaged, especially for first time memoir readers. This story also further educated me on the struggles of growing up in two different minority groups, being a queer black man in a state and an era that was not the most accepting of either of those things. Overall a very engaging and moving book, 5 stars for me.
