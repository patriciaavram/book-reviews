Artemis

by Andy Weir

Rating: DNF

Summary

Jazz Bashara is a criminal. Well, sort of. Life on Artemis, the first and only city on the moon, is tough if you're not a rich tourist or an eccentric billionaire. So smuggling in the occasional harmless bit of contraband barely counts, right? Not when you've got debts to pay and your job as a porter barely covers the rent. Everything changes when Jazz sees the chance to commit the perfect crime, with a reward too lucrative to turn down. But pulling off the impossible is just the start of her problems, as she learns that she's stepped square into a conspiracy for control of Artemis itself—and that now, her only chance at survival lies in a gambit even riskier than the first.

Artemis - Goodreads

My Thoughts

DNF at 30%. I was not impressed at all with this read, heavily disliked the writing and the storyline hadn't actually started a third of the way through. Most of the time it felt like reading an engineering textbook, which made me feel like Weir just likes dumping scientific information to seem smart. I'm not sure why he thinks readers want to read almost 100 pages straight on the composition of the bubbles that envelop each part of the city, or on how the EVAs are made, or on what each tool that Jazz finds is made and how it's used. This is similar to some of my thoughts about Project Hail Mary, and it turns out this might just be how he writes. I was also not a fan of how he wrote Jazz, almost like he's never met or interacted with a real woman before, and everything he knows about them comes from his own imagination. He should stick to writing male main characters because Ryland Grace in Project Hail Mary was much more tolerable and likeable than Jazz, regardless of the impact of his mission. Frankly, I don't have much to say about this book, it was drab and uninspiring. However, I will give The Martian a try.
