For One More Day

by Mitch Albom

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

As a child, Charley "Chick" Benetto was told by his father, "You can be a mama's boy or a daddy's boy, but you can't be both." So he chooses his father, only to see the man disappear when Charley is on the verge of adolescence. Decades later, Charley is a broken man. His life has been crumbled by alcohol and regret. He loses his job. He leaves his family. He hits bottom after discovering his only daughter has shut him out of her wedding. And he decides to take his own life. He makes a midnight ride to his small hometown, with plans to do himself in. But upon failing even to do that, he staggers back to his old house, only to make an astonishing discovery. His mother, who died eight years earlier, is still living there, and welcomes him home as if nothing ever happened.. What follows is the one "ordinary" day so many of us yearn for, a chance to make good with a lost parent, to explain the family secrets, and to seek forgiveness. Somewhere between this life and the next, Charley learns the astonishing things he never knew about his mother and her sacrifices. And he tries, with her tender guidance, to put the crumbled pieces of his life back together.

My Thoughts

First of all, Mitch Albom's writing style is very similar in both books I recently read: the distinct chapters telling the actual story, followed by some flashback chapters that explain one or more events that explain Chick's behaviour. Albom also really likes to tell instead of showing, he loves elaborating on the emotions crossing a character's face, or the specific events leading to Charley feeling a certain way. This is partially the reason why I think his books are easy to read, and not very challenging, albeit emotional at times. I feel that his stories are meant to be taken lightly, enjoyed on a rainy day while cuddled up in blankets, rather than equipped with a pencil and annotating heavily. Anyway, I found this story quite endearing, even though Charley was obnoxious at times, his mother was lovely and in my opinion, was a main character of this book as well. Charley of course, was simply behaving as most teenagers do towards their parents well into his adulthood, but that didn't make his actions understandable. Clearly, spending one more day with his mother and talking to her about the key events in her life, such as her divorce and his own shortcomings in his marital and home life, changed something in him and made him face the consequences of his actions, and thankfully he was able to rectify his relationship with his daughter as well, and be for her the father he never had for himself.
