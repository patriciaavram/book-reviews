Project Hail Mary

by Andy Weir

Rating: ★★★★☆

Summary

Ryland Grace is the sole survivor on a desperate, last-chance mission—and if he fails, humanity and the earth itself will perish. Except that right now, he doesn’t know that. He can’t even remember his own name, let alone the nature of his assignment or how to complete it. All he knows is that he’s been asleep for a very, very long time. And he’s just been awakened to find himself millions of miles from home, with nothing but two corpses for company. His crewmates dead, his memories fuzzily returning, Ryland realizes that an impossible task now confronts him. Hurtling through space on this tiny ship, it’s up to him to puzzle out an impossible scientific mystery—and conquer an extinction-level threat to our species. And with the clock ticking down and the nearest human being light-years away, he’s got to do it all alone. Or does he?

My Thoughts

First of all, Rocky is the cutest alien that has ever been written in history. I really liked this entire story and thoughts that a lot of aspects of his travels in space were really original. It felt as every discovery in space was actually pretty complex and probably took a lot of imagination to build around them. For instance, finding Xenonite and investigating how it works and its interactions with Taumoeba, as well as the process of figuring out the ecosystem surrounding Astrophage. Coming up with an alien, coming up with its biology and how it relates to the environment on its home planet was incredibly clever and I'm sure it took an insane amount of research. Describing the inner workings of a spaceship, complete with technology that doesn't exist yet like the coma-induced machine that he used, must've been a feat within itself. I really loved being in Ryland's head while he was solving the different problems that he ran into, seeing his thought process when figuring out the inner workings of Astrophage and Taumoeba was extremely interesting, and I'll give it to Weir that this part of the story was very well developed.

On the characters side, I don't really have much to say about the supporting characters, I felt that the rare times when other characters were in the plot, they were quite flat and uninteresting. Again, maybe it was done this way in order to put more emphasis on the main character but all the different people we meet through Dr.Stratt, the other two astronauts that were sent on the Hail Mary felt very caricaturized: the russian woman who drinks vodka, the asian man with the prominent accent, I mean... really? Weir could've done so much more with theses characters and having Ryland be extremely sentimental about their deaths when we know so little about them and their contribution to the Hail Mary felt again, very weird. To add insult to injury, the whole "Average man that only has basic knoweldge on astrophysics being picked to lead this mission" bit simply something that I could not get around to understanding, because let's be honest, this would not happen in a million years in real life.

Unfortunately I feel like the book could've been shortened 50 pages. There were a lot of unnecessary passages where Ryland was dumping scientific facts that werent relevant to the story, or even to the ongoing science experiments. This book was definitely meant to be introspective and maybe some people really do think that much and go on tangents when they do science experiments, I don't really know how the brain works. I also thought Ryland was a goofy character but some of his lingo took me a while to get used to, all the "What the heck"s and the "Gosh darn it"s were corny at the beginning. Also, the general vibe I felt from this novel was that Andy Weir wanted to put on display all his knowledge about space and sometimes going on irrelevant tangents, it didn't really impede my reading but at times it felt like reading a physics manual at school. The pace of this story was quite slow: It took forever to actually get into it, and the middle part of the novel when the researchers were discovering things about the Astrophages ran a bit long, but the last couple of chapters were enticing and exactly what I was looking for in a climax. The ending however, felt predictable and I would've actually been more surprised if Ryland had actually gone back to Earth. Overall, this was a very good story, I'm definitely excited to see the movie and see how they adapted the introspectiveness of it to work with the visuals. The prose and pacing left something to be desired, but I do want to read other books by this author to see if this is simply his writing style or if there is more to him.
