Chlorine

by Jade Song

Rating: ★★★☆☆

Summary

Ren Yu is a swimmer. Her daily life starts and ends with the pool. Her teammates are her only friends. Her coach, her guiding light. If she swims well enough, she will be scouted, get a scholarship, go to a good school. Her parents will love her. Her coach will be kind to her. She will have a good life. But these are human concerns. These are the concerns of those confined to land, those with legs. Ren grew up on stories of creatures of the deep, of the oceans and the rivers. Ones that called sailors to their doom. Ones that dragged them down and drowned them. Ones that feasted on their flesh. Ones of the creature that she's always longed to become: mermaid. Ren aches to be in the water. She dreams of the scent of chlorine--the feel of it on her skin. And she will do anything she can to make a life for herself where she can be free. No matter the pain. No matter what anyone else thinks. No matter how much blood she has to spill.

Chlorine - Goodreads

My Thoughts

This was definitely a very odd book, but I really liked the premise of it. I went in blind, thinking that Ren would metaphorically reach flow state and mentally transform into a mermaid, but to my shock there were actually very graphic depictions of self harm that unfortunately bumped down my enjoyment of this book, as I couldn't get through the parts depicting self-mutilation like that. From an external point of view, I understand that it did allow for a much bigger plot twist than expected, having thought that her transformation was more mental than anything else.

The writing in this novel was absolutely gorgeous, and I especially enjoyed the written letters from Cathy to the post-transformed Ren, they just showcase such a deep level of understanding and acceptance towards someone and I was extremely touched. The plot spoke to me so deeply, as I've been in Ren's shoes as an immigrant child myself, wanting to be the best swimmer on the team, to become a mermaid as well, to please my family and my team. I relate to her, in many ways and I feel like this story needed to be written, and Jade Song executed it very well. Overall, a great book, however the graphic scenes were a bit much for me.
