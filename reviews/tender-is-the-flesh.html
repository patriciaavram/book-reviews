Tender is the Flesh

by Augustina Bazterrica

Rating: ★★★☆☆

Summary

Working at the local processing plant, Marcos is in the business of slaughtering humans—though no one calls them that anymore. His wife has left him, his father is sinking into dementia, and Marcos tries not to think too hard about how he makes a living. After all, it happened so quickly. First, it was reported that an infectious virus has made all animal meat poisonous to humans. Then governments initiated the “Transition.” Now, eating human meat—“special meat”—is legal. Marcos tries to stick to numbers, consignments, processing. Then one day he’s given a a live specimen of the finest quality. Though he’s aware that any form of personal contact is forbidden on pain of death, little by little he starts to treat her like a human being. And soon, he becomes tortured by what has been lost—and what might still be saved.

My Thoughts

I thought this novel was very well written, it simply felt a bit flat to me. The plot was quite original and felt like something that would actually happen but the execution was a miss for me. I had a hard time feeling any kind of empathy for the main character, even though his story is tragic - the loss of his son and his father falling into cognitive decline is extremely sad, but nonetheless I felt that his relationships were uninteresting.

To me, this feels a lot like the plot of Fahrenheit 451: the main character has a job that is deemed honorable in the standards of that time and in the context of this animal virus that made humans need to adapt quite quickly, but he slowly realizes that his job is not necessarily morally correct when it begins to splatter into his own life. Similarly to Guy Montag, he decides to rebel in a way, and he does it by hiding the human he has impregnated in his house against society's rules. Afterwards, when his wife decides to come back to him after seeing the son that the "live specimen" had created, he gets rid of the surrogate and decides to go back to that life that he had hidden from. On the other hand, Guy Montag had rebelled simply by trying to escape this society and building his own collection of knowledge with others that have escaped. I thought this was quite an interesting story, but as I said, some parts felt uninteresting to read. Overall a decent novel though.
