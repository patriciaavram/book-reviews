Self-Portraits

by Osamu Dazai

Rating: ★★★★☆ (4.0)

Summary

A rich boy turned drop-out, a radical turned drug addict, obsessed with self destruction and suicide, Osamu Dazai retains his cult status among Japan's intellectual youth more than forty years after his death. These stories, based on his own experiences and arranged chronologically, provide insight into the sources of Dazai's enduring appeal as well as his art.

My Thoughts

Unfortunately Osamu Dazai's beautiful and lyrical prose wasn't enough for me to overlook the misogynistic and violent stories in this collection. The author is very self-obsessed and somehow simultaneously had too much ego and none at all. His back and forths between "I'm worthless, I'm a nuisance to my family and friends and I'd be better off ending my life," and "Maybe life is beautiful, and I'll try to make the best of it, to write and to see friends," was very much giving me whiplash. Obviously the author's life was tragic all throughout and he had a lot of issues, but I found his writing very beautiful overall. It's my favourite thing about reading his stories, moreso than the actual content of the novel, but his prose is so unique and really makes you think about how fleeting life is, and how mental health genuinely puts so many barriers that prevent people from reaching their goals. His heavy anxiety, and depression is extremely sad to read about and at times this book was very heavy to read, albeit still worth it because of the completely opposite mood beautiful chapters in this book. However, I would like to mention the heavy misogynistic and pedophilic story in this collection of stories, which I thought was completely unecessary. Dazai's view on women is clearly very influenced by his misfortune and awkwardness around and with women, and this feeling of worthlessness is projected upon all these women when they do something he doesn't like. This is what I mean when I say that this man has such a big ego but such a fragile ego as well, as it gets wounded extremely easily when anything doesn't do his way. He reminds me a little of Holden Caufield, in the novel **The Catcher in the Rye**, who is a very unlikeable character who is annoyed by everything that happens in his life, and who finds offense in everything as well, and thinks that everything that happens is a personal attack onto him. Overall, I'm torn in my rating. On the one side, his writing is on the more lyrical side that I've read, which I loved, but some of the topics addressed in this collection of stories was questionable. 4 stars.
