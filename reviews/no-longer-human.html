No Longer Human

by Osamu Dazai

Rating: ★★★★⯪

Summary

Osamu Dazai's No Longer Human, this leading postwar Japanese writer's second novel, tells the poignant and fascinating story of a young man who is caught between the breakup of the traditions of a northern Japanese aristocratic family and the impact of Western ideas. In consequence, he feels himself "disqualified from being human" (a literal translation of the Japanese title).

No Longer Human - Goodreads

My Thoughts

Quite honestly, the writing in this novel was exquisite. Yozo's experience as a child and growing up feels like the autistic child experience. Feeling like you don't have a place in society, or that you don't react to certain situations like neurotypical people would, is a very real experience that makes it harder for children to discover themselves in the long run, as I noticed by his toxic habit of going back to the same friend that does him wrong, simply because it is familiar. His feeling of behaving like the class clown in order for his classmates to like him seems like the behaviour of someone who is not able to recognize social cues and who is overcompensating by putting on a character or a show. Overall, I found this story extremely captivating, I actually could not put this book down and felt extremely immersed in the story. This is a book about vicious cycles and clearly depicts how hard it is to fall out of a cycle once you have gotten used to it, in Yozo's case, it was alcohol, and women. A wonderful but depressing read.
